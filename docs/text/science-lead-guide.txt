Updated scientific working guide for docs/text/science-lead-guide.txt

PURPOSE
Use this guide to keep the concept report scientifically defensible. The habitat and organisms are fictional; the physical and biochemical mechanisms are grounded in real observations where cited. Separate evidence, model assumptions, and untested biological hypotheses. Do not treat this working guide as a final formatted four-page submission.

LOCKED CREATIVE DECISIONS (PRESERVED)
- Moon: Pyrothalasa, fictional icy moon inspired by Enceladus, orbiting Saturn.
- Habitat: one field of Lost City-like white carbonate chimneys at 50 km below the surface (20 km ice + 30 km ocean).
- Energy pathway: water-rock reactions (serpentinization) supply H₂; methanogenic microorganisms use hydrogen and available inorganic carbon.
- Oxygen: assume negligible free dissolved oxygen everywhere in the modelled habitat; absence of sunlight alone DOES NOT prove oxygen is absent.
- Three levels of life: methanogens → fictional anaerobic single-celled grazers → fictional microscopic Shadowfin (<1 mm).
- Important biological qualification: grazers are proposed to use suitable bacteria within a wider microbial community, not automatically methanogenic archaea as sole food. The wider community must itself depend on primary production and recycling; it is not an extra magical energy source.
- Three levels remain the presentation model. Extra microbial diversity within the base does not become an additional illustrated trophic level unless deliberately justified.
- All energy coins, some gauge values, and organism traits are illustrative assumptions.

A. HABITAT AND CONDITIONS (REPORT SECTION 4a)

1. Depth: 50 km under surface: 20 km ice, 30 km water. These exact thicknesses are fictional choices informed by Enceladus observations (Thomas et al., 2016; NASA).
2. Pressure: approximately 56 bar at the floor. Simplified calculation:
   p ≈ (ρ_ice × g × h_ice) + (ρ_water × g × h_water)
   ≈ (920 × 0.113 × 20,000 + 1,030 × 0.113 × 30,000) Pa
   ≈ 5.57 × 10^6 Pa ≈ 55.7 bar.
   Assumes constant g, representative densities and negligible surface pressure; pressure is not a direct measurement. Earth comparison is roughly 550 m underwater.
3. Temperature: dark ocean near freezing; 'garden water' around 8 °C is an assumed locally warmed mixing zone. Lost City-like vent fluid broadly around 40–90 °C (Kelley et al., 2005). Do not imply organisms inhabit all these temperatures simultaneously.
4. Salinity: salty ocean, analogous to salts identified in Enceladus' plume (Postberg et al., 2009).
5. Alkalinity: Lost City-like vent fluid pH approximately 9–11 (Kelley et al., 2005); applying the entire range to all pores is an assumption.
6. Chemical species: H₂, methane and dissolved inorganic carbon; their quantities and spatial transport in our moon are unknown. Cassini detected H₂ in Enceladus' plume (Waite et al., 2017).
7. Light: sunlight cannot penetrate a 20 km ice shell; the exact visual fade rate in the website is illustrative.
8. Dissolved oxygen: negligible by MODEL ASSUMPTION. Photosynthesis is absent at depth, but abiotic oxygen formation and transport cannot be categorically ruled out.
9. Food from above: assume no photosynthesis-derived marine snow. Do not categorically assert nothing else could grow anywhere else in the ocean.

READY-TO-USE HABITAT PARAGRAPH
The Hydrogen Garden is one field of white hydrothermal chimneys on the seafloor of Pyrothalasa, a fictional icy moon. Our chosen habitat lies 50 km beneath the surface, under 20 km of ice and 30 km of salty ocean. A simplified hydrostatic calculation gives about 56 bar at the seafloor. No sunlight reaches the water, and we assume negligible free dissolved oxygen. Hydrogen-rich, alkaline fluid enters a cooler ocean through porous chimneys. The setting draws on Earth's Lost City hydrothermal field and evidence from Saturn's moon Enceladus; the exact dimensions and temperatures are our design assumptions.

B. THE CHEMICAL ENERGY SOURCE (REPORT SECTION 4b)

Serpentinization: water reacts with reactive ultramafic/olivine-rich rocks and can form molecular hydrogen, providing a chemical energy source (Kelley et al., 2005). Waite et al. (2017) measured H₂ in Enceladus' plume, supporting the plausibility of chemical energy for methanogenesis there.

Methanogenesis reaction:
CO₂ + 4H₂ → CH₄ + 2H₂O

This is a balanced chemical reaction; methane and water are products, and the cells can conserve some released energy for growth. Taubner et al. (2018) demonstrated methane production by an Earth methanogen in some simulated Enceladus-like conditions. That study does not automatically verify survival at every chosen pH, temperature, pressure and resource concentration at once.

CARBON LIMITATION — EXPLAIN PRECISELY
- 'Dissolved inorganic carbon' includes dissolved CO₂, bicarbonate and carbonate.
- In alkaline water (pH 9–11), bicarbonate/carbonate can dominate over free dissolved CO₂.
- We assume circulation brings a sufficient accessible carbon source into warm, hydrogen-rich chimney pores and that suitable microbes can use it.
- This assumption needs testing. Hydrogen availability alone is not proof of sustainable biomass production.
- Lost City's abiotic hydrocarbon chemistry should not be simplified as seawater CO₂ freely reaching all vent pores (Proskurowski et al., 2008).

SUSTAINABILITY
Tidal heating and water-rock reactions could sustain an energy supply over substantial periods, depending on orbital, geological and permeability conditions (Choblet et al., 2017). Do not write an exact lifespan for our vent system unless directly supported by the model and clearly qualified.

READY-TO-USE ENERGY PARAGRAPH
Water reacting with olivine-rich rock can release hydrogen through serpentinization. At Earth's Lost City vents, comparable rock-water chemistry supplies reduced chemicals, and Cassini found hydrogen in Enceladus' plume (Kelley et al., 2005; Waite et al., 2017). Methanogens can use hydrogen and available inorganic carbon to make methane while conserving energy for growth: CO₂ + 4H₂ → CH₄ + 2H₂O. Laboratory studies show this metabolism can function in some Enceladus-like conditions (Taubner et al., 2018). Our key uncertainty is whether enough biologically usable inorganic carbon reaches suitable chimney pores, especially under very alkaline conditions.

C. DESIGN-CHOICE EVIDENCE TABLE (REPORT SECTION 4c)

DECISION 1 — Hydrogen from water reacting with rock
Evidence: hydrogen-rich Lost City chemistry (Kelley et al., 2005) and Enceladus H₂ observations (Waite et al., 2017).
Assumption: active water circulation through reactive rock persists on Pyrothalasa and supplies adequate H₂.

DECISION 2 — Methanogens form the primary-production base
Evidence: methane-related microbes at Lost City (Kelley et al., 2005); experimental methanogenesis under some simulated Enceladus-like conditions (Taubner et al., 2018).
Assumption: organisms exist, suitable temperatures overlap with hydrogen and usable inorganic carbon, and net biomass production is sufficient. Habitable ≠ inhabited.

DECISION 3 — One dark, salty ocean vent field
Evidence: Enceladus global ocean, sodium salts and silica observations (Thomas et al., 2016; Postberg et al., 2009; Hsu et al., 2015).
Assumption: the fictional moon shares suitable structural and chemical properties; we focus on one field, not a planet-wide claim.

DECISION 4 — Three microscopic anaerobic levels
Evidence: anaerobic protists can consume certain bacteria (Hirakata et al., 2020). Permanently anaerobic Loricifera were proposed by Danovaro et al. (2010), questioned by Bernhard et al. (2015) and defended by Danovaro et al. (2016).
Assumption: suitable bacteria and grazing organisms are supported by the microbial community, and a fictional predator can survive and reproduce without oxygen. This is the weakest part of the model.

DECISION 5 — A short illustrated food web
Evidence: energy is not completely transferred between trophic levels; ecological transfer efficiencies vary. Pauly & Christensen (1995) is an ecosystem-level reference, not a measured efficiency for Pyrothalasa.
Assumption: '1,000 → 100 → 10 energy coins' uses illustrative 10% transfer each step. Avoid presenting the remainder as exclusively heat or predicting that every anaerobic animal must be tiny.

DECISION 6 — Long-lived hydrothermal activity
Evidence: prolonged tidal-heating/hydrothermal mechanisms have been modelled for Enceladus (Choblet et al., 2017).
Assumption: analogous conditions persist on Pyrothalasa.

D. FOOD WEB AND NUTRITION (REPORT SECTION 4d)

Level 1 — Methane-makers: anaerobic archaea that conserve chemical energy from H₂ and inorganic carbon, producing biomass. Methanogenesis is a metabolism, not proof of food availability to every consumer.

Supporting microbial community: a proposed group of bacteria able to use locally generated and recycled organic material. This group is NOT an independent energy source. To be credible, the report must acknowledge that its food and maintenance depend on primary production and nutrient recycling. We do not claim an established community of this exact composition exists at Pyrothalasa.

Level 2 — Grazers: fictional ciliated, single-celled anaerobic organisms proposed to feed on suitable bacterial biomass. Hirakata et al. (2020) found anaerobic protists could ingest/digest some bacteria, but the tested methanogenic archaea did not support their growth. Therefore do NOT say 'grazers definitely eat methanogens whole' as an established fact.

Level 3 — Shadowfin: fictional eyeless, sub-millimetre predator with a protective shell and movement/chemical senses. It hypothetically eats the grazers. It is inspired by Loricifera but their permanently anaerobic lifestyle is scientifically disputed; no source proves Shadowfin's oxygen-free predation, reproduction or energy balance.

Energy example: 1,000 → 100 → 10 illustrative 'coins'. Each arrow assumes 10% transfer; other energy goes into metabolism, heat, waste or material not consumed at that level. This is NOT a measurement or fixed ecological law.

When hydrogen decreases: expect reduced primary-production potential, which could limit food for grazers and Shadowfin. Timing, extinction, adaptation, migration and recolonization are unknown.

E. REJECTED ALTERNATIVE (CREATIVITY AND JUSTIFICATION)

We considered an Earth-like black-smoker community with large tubeworms relying on sulfide-oxidizing symbionts. Familiar oxygen-using sulfur-oxidation reactions depend on oxygen supplied to Earth's deep waters ultimately through photosynthesis. We chose hydrogen-powered methanogenesis because it can operate without oxygen, and deliberately modelled our habitat with negligible dissolved oxygen. We do NOT claim darkness chemically guarantees zero oxygen or that every sulfur-based metabolism requires oxygen. This is an evidence-informed design choice, not a universal ban on sulfide chemistry.

F. VALIDATION AND LIMITATIONS (EVIDENCE LEAD — AHMAD)

PRIMARY LIMITATIONS
1. Inorganic carbon availability: extreme alkalinity reduces free CO₂, and the concentration/transport/use of carbon in chimney pores are unknown.
2. Grazer diet: tested methanogenic archaea did not support growth in the anaerobic protists examined by Hirakata et al. (2020). A wider suitable bacterial diet is a hypothesis requiring an independent energy and carbon budget.
3. Shadowfin: permanently anaerobic Loricifera interpretation is disputed; Shadowfin's metabolism, feeding and reproduction remain speculation.
4. Energy transfer: hydrogen flux, microbial productivity, recycling and consumer demands have not been quantified. The 10% coin rule is only pedagogical.
5. Physical simplifications: constant gravity, fixed density, 8 °C garden water, ice temperature profile, approximate light attenuation and negligible free oxygen are assumptions.

PROPOSED TEST A — Methanogen feasibility
What: Can an appropriate Earth methanogen grow with the garden's proposed resource supply?
Who: Qualified microbiology lab.
How: Compare controlled environments that separately and jointly represent temperature, pressure, alkaline pH, hydrogen supply and dissolved inorganic carbon availability. Track cell biomass and methane; include suitable controls for non-biological methane.
Success: Reproducible biomass growth and methane formation at plausible garden-like substrate levels.
Limits: Validates only the primary producer and selected tested conditions; not an alien biosphere.

PROPOSED TEST B — Grazer nutrition
What: Can anaerobic protists maintain growth on food the garden could produce?
Who: Microbial ecology lab.
How: Compare methanogen-only biomass, an ecologically justified mixed microbial food source and controls. Measure feeding, cell growth and reproduction.
Success: Sustained reproduction on an energetically plausible microbial community.
Limits: Does not validate Shadowfin.

PROPOSED TEST C — Food-web energy budget
What: Could a third trophic level receive enough energy?
Who: Ecology modelling team.
How: Model H₂ flux → methanogen biomass → available bacterial prey → grazer biomass → hypothetical predator needs, varying uncertain efficiencies.
Success: A positive energy balance over explicitly stated, plausible parameter ranges.
Limits: Computer modelling tests plausibility only; not existence or evolution.

POSSIBLE SPACECRAFT OBSERVATION
Measuring H₂, methane and carbon-bearing species in plumes could constrain the underlying chemical energy supply. Such measurements are not proof of biology on their own.

G. COMMUNICATION RULES AND TEAM HANDOFFS

- All claims in the final four-page report must appear in the evidence/assumptions master table and link to sources.
- Use the labels EVIDENCE, CALCULATION, ASSUMPTION or FICTION accurately.
- No categorical 'no sunlight → no oxygen'.
- No categorical 'methanogens feed grazers' without a tested or clearly assumed pathway.
- No categorical 'Loricifera prove Shadowfin can exist'.
- No claim of '10% always passes on' or 'all missing energy is heat'.
- Keep equations, depth, pressure, names and all three food-web levels consistent across report, video and website; editing this document DOES NOT update the website.
- Evidence lead checks references and the provenance of every numerical claim. Ecology lead checks the food-chain description. Science leads check chemistry and habitat assumptions. The report owner controls the four-page layout. Video/web owners should update their materials only when authorized.
- Log each team decision and named contribution accurately. Disclose all AI tools and their real uses; AI-generated references or claims must be checked.

OPEN ITEMS — DO NOT PRESENT AS VERIFIED RESULTS
- Quantify or at least bound hydrogen flux, accessible inorganic carbon and how biomass supports the proposed mixed bacterial community.
- Consider whether the proposed grazer could truly survive at the chosen temperature, pressure and pH.
- Determine whether a realistic consumer energy budget could support a predator; if not, keep Shadowfin as an explicitly speculative hypothetical organism.
- Confirm exact publication interpretation if discussing Choblet's timescales, rather than assigning a lifespan to the fictional moon.
- Verify final figure credits, every bibliography entry, team contributions, and the event's conflicting deadline statements.
